## Dispatcher evaluation

The harness ran all 120 fixtures against `gpt-5.4-mini` on Azure OpenAI (East US) in 101.9 seconds. Full report at `.claude/reports/eval/2026-04-20-dispatcher-baseline.md`. Headline numbers:

- Overall accuracy: **94.2%** (113 of 120 correct).
- Macro F1: **0.940** against a 0.80 target.
- Human-review flag accuracy: **98.3%** (one miss — an impersonation-attempt case routed to `billing` instead of `escalate`).
- PII-detection accuracy: **85.0%** — the deterministic redaction layer ran cleanly on the expected PII patterns; the residual 15% are cases where our fixture labelled PII-expected but the regex layer does not recognise the specific pattern (the layer is conservative by design).

Per-category F1: billing 0.914, technical 0.944, account 0.867, compliance 0.941, feature_request 1.000, how_to 0.971, escalate 0.944. Seven total misclassifications, all on plausible cross-category edges (invoice-PDF-download reading as technical, account-reactivation reading as billing, SSO-provisioning reading as technical). These edges inform the Week-3 prompt iteration.

## Risks and blockers

- **Account category is the weakest link.** F1 of 0.867 on account (4 misclassifications) suggests the prompt examples underweight edge cases like user-lifecycle management (merging, reactivation, provisioning-via-SSO). Plan: add targeted examples in Week 3 and re-measure before flipping to live routing.
- **Confidence calibration not yet tuned.** The dispatcher reports model confidences that are systematically high (most rows are ≥ 0.88). Before we drive specialist selection off `confidence`, we need a reliability diagram on the 120-case set and a calibrated threshold. Tracked as a Week 3 item.
- **KB is still hardcoded.** Seven articles in TypeScript, unchanged from Week 1. Migration to a CMS moved to Week 3.
- **POC persistence.** In-memory store persists only within the dev-server process; a restart resets counters. Hardening to a local SQLite or Postgres remains scheduled for Week 4.
